Notes on Cricket Ground Maintenance and Match Preparation
Rajiv Gandhi International Stadium, Uppal — Groundskeeping Manual (Extract)

PITCH PREPARATION

A red-soil pitch requires 12 to 14 days of preparation before a Test match. Rolling
begins 10 days out, using a 1-tonne roller for 20 minutes each morning. The final
two days use a heavy 2-tonne roller to compact the surface.

Grass is cut to 4 millimetres for Test matches and 2 millimetres for limited-overs
games. Watering stops 48 hours before play begins so that the surface hardens.

The black-soil pitches at Uppal favour spin bowling from day three onwards, whereas
red-soil pitches offer more consistent bounce throughout.

OUTFIELD AND DRAINAGE

The subsurface drainage system can clear 25 millimetres of rainfall per hour. After
heavy rain, super-soppers cover the outfield in approximately 40 minutes. The
outfield grass is Bermuda hybrid, maintained at 12 millimetres.

MATCH DAY OPERATIONS

Covers must be deployable within 90 seconds of a rain signal from the umpires.
A crew of 28 groundstaff is required for a match day. Boundary ropes are set at a
minimum of 65 metres and a maximum of 82 metres from the centre of the pitch.

Sightscreens are repositioned during the innings break, which lasts 40 minutes in
Test cricket and 30 minutes in one-day matches.

FLOODLIGHTS

The stadium has six floodlight towers, each carrying 96 lamps. Full illumination
reaches 1,400 lux at ground level, which meets ICC requirements for day-night
fixtures. Warm-up time from cold start is 15 minutes.
